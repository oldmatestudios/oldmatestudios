The Studio

Our Story

From a game jam idea to a growing studio with international partnerships. This is how OldMateStudios came to life.

Origins

Born at IHUB

OldMateStudios started where many great ideas do — at a game jam. In June 2026, a group of friends and developers gathered at Co-Creation Hub (IHUB) Nairobi for the KAS Entrepreneurship Game Jam, a 48-hour challenge co-hosted by the Konrad-Adenauer-Stiftung and Leipzig's R42 Games Hub.

The result was a rough prototype of what would become EntrePoly — and a first-place finish that proved Kenyan developers could build compelling multiplayer experiences. That weekend, we discovered something: when African talent meets global ambition, the results speak for themselves.

Growth

Building a Team

What began as a jam team quickly solidified into a six-person studio. Philip Katana handles finance and planning. Donald Bitange tests every build. Allan Robinson keeps production on track. Fred Gitonga crafts the UI/UX. Lilian Warutere drives marketing and research. Gichia builds the core game systems.

Each member brought something unique — and together, we became more than the sum of our parts. We are not a company of departments. We are six people who ship.

Culture

How We Work

We believe in flat hierarchies, radical transparency, and shipping often. Every team member has a voice in design decisions. We prototype in sprints, test with real players, and iterate based on feedback — not ego.

Our culture is built on three pillars: craftsmanship (we care about every interaction), collaboration (no one builds alone), and impact (every game should teach something worth knowing).

Future

Where We Are Going

Right now, EntrePoly is our focus — getting it from alpha to a polished release across PC, web, and mobile. We are also designing a physical board game version for classrooms and workshops.

Long-term, we want to build games that only a team with our perspective could make — experiences rooted in African contexts, built for global audiences. We are not there yet. But we are building toward it, one project at a time.

LL

Level Labs

Indie Game Studio — Leipzig, Germany

Level Labs is a Leipzig-based indie game studio founded in April 2024 by Jennifer Funke (Creative Director), Daniel Zuher (Executive Director), and Philipp Illmer (CEO). They create their own games — experimental, fun, and made with passion — alongside "playful solutions" for learning and education. Their debut title DrillScape is a 2D space-mining roguelike where you control a speedy rover, mine deep for resources, upgrade tools, and escape before the toxic atmosphere closes in. They received the Press Start Games Grant and made their debut at Gamescom 2024.

R42 Games Hub

Games Ecosystem — Leipzig, Germany

R42 is a 7-floor gaming ecosystem in the heart of Leipzig at Ritterstraße 42, opened April 1, 2026. Founded by Christopher Siebenhüner (CEO), Tom Potutschek (COO), and Ralph Hölzer, it brings together coworking spaces, a gaming arena, a production studio, Bar42, event spaces, and even apartments — all under one roof. It hosts regular game jams (including Global Game Jam Leipzig 2026) and runs the R42 Games Accelerator for startup founders in games and media. Over 30 creative minds from Gecko.1, Gecko.2, and N!CE power the hub.

How It Started

The Game Jam Connection

June 22–24, 2026

KAS and the Leipzig-based R42 Games Hub co-hosted the Entrepreneurship Game Jam in Nairobi — a 48-hour event bringing together developers, designers, and entrepreneurs to build games with real-world impact.

Post-Jam

OldMateStudios' prototype caught the attention of the ecosystem. The intersection of multiplayer systems, educational outcomes, and African game development aligned with what partners like R42 (indie game hub) and Level Labs (indie studio) were looking to support.

Growth Phase

These partnerships opened doors — from R42's game jam network and accelerator in Leipzig to Level Labs' indie game expertise, and KAS's ongoing educational and entrepreneurship programs across Kenya.

Building in Africa's Leading Startup Ecosystem

Kenya overtook Nigeria as Africa's top venture capital destination in 2025, with Kenyan startups raising $984 million — nearly a third of all startup capital raised across the continent. Nairobi now leads the continent in dealmaking, driven by a maturing ecosystem of investors, hubs, and policy support.
